**FP Assistant v2 — assessment, 1 October 2026**

**Recommendation: proceed with a narrower pilot after revising the evidence, state and Word-update design.** The four roles, complete reading of key documents, early officer feedback, separate challenge pass and use of approved FP examples are a good foundation. The present specification is more convincing about organising the work than about proving that its explanations and investment judgments are sound. It also promises a broader Word reconciliation and cloud persistence system than “three small programs” suggests.

Three agents independently reviewed platform feasibility, current finance workflows, and implementation simplicity. I combined their findings with research on long-report analysis and financial evaluation. This is a design review, not a tenant test or a certification of writing quality. The complete execution pack, actual FP template and approved FP examples were not provided. The cited Word prototype was not present in this checkout; its reported tests remain supplied evidence, not independently reproduced results.

**Keep complete reading of Tier A, but make the evidence retained from that reading more precise.** The plan is right to protect the latest accounts and every note. Full reading can be sequential and resumable; it does not require keeping the whole report in every drafting context. My recommended workflow is:

1. Identify the document, version, reporting entity, consolidated/standalone scope, period, currency and scale. Map PDF pages to printed page numbers. Build the part map from the actual pages as well as the contents page, so an imperfect contents list cannot silently omit material.
2. Read all Tier A parts in logical sections. Approximately 15 pages is a useful working target, but preserve complete tables, their headers, footnotes and note continuations. A page ledger should distinguish read, unreadable and pending; the checklist should additionally allow unresolved. An unreadable disclosure cannot be classified as not disclosed.
3. Preserve table structure. Inspect page images or OCR where text extraction loses columns, signs, units or scanned text. Follow cross-references between the statements, notes and management commentary. Keep unresolved extraction issues visible.
4. Save material facts and claims with exact evidence locations, not just a 200-word digest. A digest helps navigate a source. Each critical amount or conclusion also needs its supporting passage/table, source version, period and entity. Source material remains the authority when summaries disagree.
5. Assemble the analysis by topic across the report: earnings quality, cash conversion, debt, liquidity, covenants, related parties, contingent obligations and subsequent events. Compare against the approved spread with matching definitions. An apparent difference can be a scope or accounting-basis difference rather than an error.
6. Draft from the resulting evidence and explanations, then reopen the original cited pages for material claims during review. Full reading addresses coverage; targeted retrieval addresses verification. Both are needed.

Promote deal-critical legal, security and E&S documents to Tier A. A3 currently treats these as Tier B by document category, even though they can determine whether the financing is acceptable. Keep the tier decision at Checkpoint 1, with promotion when new evidence changes materiality. Likewise, the 24-month timeline should be a focus, not a ban on older developments that still determine the current position.

**High-quality FPs require an explicit analytical writing standard.** A4 is directionally strong, but “movement, explanation, source” can still produce a plausible story unsupported by the evidence. For each material topic, require an observation, supported explanation, unresolved portion and financing implication. These can be compact records and prose; no new analytical platform is needed.

| Topic | What the analyst should establish before the lead writes the conclusion |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Price, volume, mix, acquisitions and FX where relevant; distinguish growth from a change in consolidation or reporting. |
| Profitability | Recurring operating drivers, exceptional items, accounting changes and the extent to which explanations reconcile the movement. |
| Cash conversion | How earnings become cash, including receivables, inventory, payables, taxes and capital expenditure; identify temporary cash benefits. |
| Liquidity | Unrestricted and accessible cash, committed facilities, draw conditions, near-term obligations and refinancing dependence. |
| Leverage and covenants | Matching debt/EBITDA definitions, leases, entity perimeter, maturity concentration, tested headroom and relevant dates. |
| Repayment and downside | The repayment source and what happens if the main assumption fails, using approved model outputs where available; missing material scenarios become explicit open items. |
| Mitigants and conditions | Whether protection exists, is legally/operationally effective, is available at the required time and adequately addresses the identified risk. |
| Investment case | Why this financing, on these terms, is defensible; development impact/additionality where required; strongest contrary evidence; residual risk and what would change the recommendation. |

An executed agreement establishes something different from management's intention to refinance. An audited inventory write-down establishes something different from management's explanation of input-cost pressure. An arithmetic bridge establishes consistency, not causation. Keep those distinctions visible in the analysis and prose.

Using the spec's illustrative margin example, a defensible paragraph would be: “Gross margin fell four percentage points, from 22% to 18%. Note 14 records an inventory write-down; management also attributes the decline to higher milk prices. The contribution of each driver has not been reconciled. The repayment case should therefore not depend on full margin recovery without further evidence.” This is an example of the writing standard, not a finding about an actual deal.

The recommendation entered at Checkpoint 1 should be provisional. The grill should have access to the original evidence and a specific duty to identify what would overturn the case. It should test the strongest contrary interpretation, residual risk and suitability of proposed conditions. One challenge round plus a recheck is a sensible default; unresolved material findings determine whether another round is needed.

Do not make every judgment a script rule. Scripts can enforce unique IDs, required metadata, arithmetic, source-location presence and state transitions. The model and officer must judge evidential support, causal plausibility, materiality and adequacy of protection. “All judgment lives in the skills” means the policy is readable; it does not mean the judgment becomes deterministic.

**A simpler first implementation can preserve the user experience.** Keep the four named roles as sequential skills and retain the three script responsibilities. Use the existing B7 fallback proactively: store keyed narrative sections in an Analysis sheet in the canonical register, and generate the readable Analysis.md from that revision. Since the spec says the officer reads rather than edits the workbook, this removes a second mutable authority without introducing a database or a new JSON state format.

The minimal state arrangement is:

- The register owns structured records, narrative sections, evidence references, section baselines and current output references. Each completed revision is a new file.
- Analysis.md is a readable generated view carrying its register revision. A failed export can be regenerated.
- Word is the editable handoff. Subsequent officer edits are inputs to the next round; they do not invalidate the historical register revision.
- The next completed register revision points to a Word output only after that output has been saved and verified. A failed run may leave an unused draft, but must not silently promote it to current.
- Fresh tasks resume from the latest valid completed revision, not merely the highest filename. Keep one writer; versioning is recovery discipline, not concurrent-write protection.

Ready applies to the recorded Word content hash and evidence revision. A subsequent Word edit or pending source-impact assessment invalidates current readiness until reconciled and reviewed. Historical register revisions remain intact, but they cannot certify newer content.

If direct human editing of Analysis.md later becomes a requirement, revisit this choice. For the present user workflow, a second authoritative prose store adds coordination work with little benefit.

For Word, use whole-section baselines that account for structure and formatting, not only normalised visible text. Freeze sections with officer edits, comments, tracked changes or ambiguous ownership. Offer the replacement separately for officer review, using Word Compare if useful. This retains the ability to improve the FP while deferring arbitrary paragraph merging. It is a deliberate reduction in automation, not a claim that all Word structures are now easy.

Do not add parallel writers, a graph database, a generic agent orchestration framework, a new calculation engine or a broad document-management system to v1. Explicit deal links are sufficient initially. Fine-grained dependency invalidation, automated deal discovery and paragraph merging can follow demonstrated need. Keep shared skill rules in one source during development and copy them at packaging time to avoid drift.

**Build and evaluate one complete slice before building the whole system.** I would change B9 to the following sequence:

1. In the actual tenant, prove fresh-task file access and a script-produced file round-trip: read, hash, modify, save and independently read back. Test the real template and labels. A Python import test alone does not answer the critical question.
2. Produce one financial-analysis section and the recommendation/main risk section from a real historical report and approved spread. Include a note-derived risk and a management explanation. Run the grill against original sources. Have the officer judge substance and writing before expanding automation.
3. Exercise officer edits, comments, a new adverse source, an interrupted publication and a retry. Verify preserved content, stale-state detection, recovery and refusal to mark incomplete work Ready.
4. Replay at least three deliberately different deals before relying on the general workflow: a relatively straightforward case; a case with restatement, scope or currency complexity; and a distressed or materially adverse case. Use the approved FPs as comparators, with independently reviewed expected findings. Do not feed a replay's target FP back as a style exemplar or current-deal source when measuring a fresh drafting task.
5. Run one live officer-supervised pilot. Measure total time to an acceptable FP, officer edit time, material errors/omissions, technical failures and actual Cowork credits. Record model variant, effort, plugin version and source versions.

Suggested acceptance gates are zero material wrong-entity/period/unit errors, zero unsupported decision-changing assertions, all expected critical issues found or explicitly left unresolved, correct financial-table transfer and calculations, no lost officer edits, and no silent partial-state promotion. Review every decision-critical claim against the original evidence; use sampling for lower-impact details. A small pilot passing these gates supports a pilot conclusion, not an across-all-deals guarantee.

Score prose separately on clarity of recommendation, explanation of drivers, connection to repayment and downside, treatment of uncertainty, house style and concision. Use at least one experienced officer or credit reviewer who did not draft the output. A model grading its own draft is not enough. Keep the final whole-FP consistency check even when style edits touch only changed sections.

The 1,500-line target and B6 token/time savings should remain hypotheses. A line count cannot measure Word edge cases, cloud integration or semantic reliability. Re-measure the complete workload, including the protected full read, on the exact tenant model. Report cost per acceptable FP and officer effort saved. That is the implementation and quality evidence needed to decide whether this assistant is worth expanding.
